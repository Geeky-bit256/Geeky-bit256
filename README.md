# Hi, I'm Walter 👋

## About Me

I'm a developer who enjoys solving business problems through technology. I believe in understanding a problem before jumping into code, questioning assumptions, and using critical and analytical thinking to identify practical solutions.

I value evidence over assumptions, continuous learning, and evaluating whether the solutions I build actually solve the problems they were intended to address.

I started learning programming because I wanted to understand how software systems work and communicate with one another. I was also interested in automating repetitive tasks and building my own applications to solve problems.

## My Development Philosophy

I believe understanding the problem is one of the most important parts of software development.

Before building anything, I want to understand:
- Who the users are.
- What they need.
- What problem we're trying to solve.
- Whether building a solution is actually necessary.

From there, I consider the tools and technologies that can help me deliver the most appropriate solution.

## How I Learn and Solve Problems

I approach learning and problem-solving by first understanding what I'm trying to achieve and why it matters.

When exploring a new technology, I first ask what problem it solves and whether I actually need it for the task at hand.

I then work towards understanding the problem deeply, defining what the desired outcome should look like, and breaking the work into smaller, manageable tasks.

After implementation, I reflect on what worked, what didn't, and what I can improve.

## My Relationship With AI

I view AI as a tool and thinking partner that supports my development process.

I use it to accelerate tasks I'm already familiar with, explore unfamiliar concepts, challenge my assumptions, and generate possible solutions.

AI can help with repetitive and boilerplate code, as well as implementation tasks where the expected inputs and outputs are understood. However, I believe the developer remains responsible for understanding and evaluating the generated output.

That means questioning whether the solution is correct, secure, maintainable, and aligned with business requirements. It also means evaluating trade-offs, making architectural decisions, understanding potential risks, and measuring whether the final solution delivers meaningful results.

I believe AI should strengthen a developer's thinking, not replace it.
